whenbee.app
01
The problem

A large, self-diagnosed audience is wrong about time. Consistently.

  • "Fifteen minutes" is really forty. Every time. It does not improve on its own.
  • The plan collapses by 11am and the day becomes apology management.
  • Known as time blindness in ADHD, and the planning fallacy in everyone else.
  • No market education to fund. People search these exact words, so acquisition can run through store search instead of paid media.
Voice of customer · live review mining
"I'm always late and people think I'm lazy. I just want to know how long things actually take me."
Recurring phrasing across ADHD app reviews and Reddit, May 2026 research pass.
Problem
02
Why nobody has fixed it

Three product categories, one shared blind spot.

AI planners
Guess for an average person
"This takes about 25 minutes." Exactly as wrong on task 100 as on task 1. No feedback loop, so accuracy never compounds.
Time trackers
Report the gap, then drop it
"Planned 30, spent 52." Accurate and inert. Nothing carries that error into tomorrow's estimate.
Planning platforms
Trust the broken input
"Your day is overbooked", based on numbers the user typed. Those numbers are the defect.

All three treat the user's estimate as data. Whenbee treats it as a symptom and measures the error. One indie app (TimeNinja, launched 2026) has started down the same road, which is covered head-to-head on slide 9.

Problem
03
The solution

One loop. Four seconds of user effort a day.

01
Guess
"Emails, 15 min." One tap on a rough-time chip. No forms, no setup.
02
Start
One tap. The timer is the log, so there is nothing to remember afterwards.
03
Learn
On stop, the engine updates a per-category bias multiplier from the real number.
04
Correct
Next time they guess 15, the app shows ~25. Their number, everywhere they plan.

About forty lines of deterministic math running on the phone, offline. No LLM in the loop means no inference bill, no latency, no privacy surface, and an identical answer every time.

05
The product, in a normal Tuesday

Four surfaces. No inbox to maintain.

Morning
Today
Each task shows an honest length and a finish time. The day fails on screen before it fails in life.
Working
Timer
A calm ring and "done about 10:09". Amber when it runs long. Never red, never a scold.
Deadline
Start-by plan
"Leave by 4:05, or drop one thing." Works backwards through measured durations, then ranks what to cut.
Payoff
Patterns
"You run almost 2× over on writing." Compounding self-knowledge, banked into a growing collection.

Three independent reasons to reopen the app: the daily plan (habit), the patterns (curiosity), and an automatic nudge when the user's pace shifts (zero effort). Missing a day severs none of them.

Product
06
Defensibility

The moat compounds on the user's phone.

Cold start
Accurate on day one
Population priors give a real number before the first log. The ADHD complaint is that they cannot estimate, so every rival that says "learns over weeks" still asks the user to do the one thing they cannot do.
Data
Compounding and personal
Every log sharpens one user's model, per category, on ad-hoc tasks rather than fixed routine steps. A copied interface still starts every user at zero. Marginal cost is about zero, so margin sits near 85%.
Trust
No guilt mechanics, ever
No streaks, no red, nothing that can be lost. The nearest rival ships a streak counter, the exact loss-frame that drives this audience away. Also: no account, no cloud, no LLM, so no breach surface.

Honest caveat: the mechanism is not patent-protected and one indie rival is building toward it. The defensible part is the combination, cold-start accuracy plus a per-category continuous model plus a no-guilt design, on two platforms. Speed and execution carry the rest.

Competition
08
The closest competitor

TimeNinja, honestly assessed.

Where they are ahead
Shipped first (v1.4, July 2026), cheaper on annual and lifetime, a strong research-SEO content engine, Family Mode for parents, and on-device AI task breakdown. Solo developer, shipping fast.
Where we are ahead
Accurate on day one instead of "over weeks". Learns ad-hoc tasks, not only routine steps. A whole-day feasibility verdict. No streak counter. And Android, which they do not have.
The shared truth
Neither app has traction. TimeNinja shows zero ratings on the App Store today. The category leader has not been decided, and the first product with real reviews takes the position.
The others
Llama Life ($6/mo) shows the overrun but never learns it. Tiimo (~$7.99/mo, 500k users, Apple award) sells generic AI estimates. Sunsama and Motion defend calendar capacity at $15–29/mo, a different buyer entirely.

Named risk, stated plainly: TimeNinja closes the gap by adding day-one estimates, or Tiimo bolts a learning layer onto 500k existing users. Defense is shipping the cold-start advantage and the no-guilt stance before either moves, and being on both platforms while they are not.

Business model

Free forever: the measuring machine. Paid: what you do with it.

Free · $0
The entire calibration product
  • Unlimited logging, every category, full depth
  • The honest number everywhere they plan
  • Progress system, companion, start-by planner
  • Personal patterns and discoveries
Pro · the payoff bundle
Eleven compounding features
  • PDF report for a coach, clinician or self
  • Weekly and monthly honest review ritual

The rule: charge for the payoff, never for the sensor. Capping logging would stop the product learning and destroy the reason to pay. The free tier is also the trial, which is what a subscription-burned audience responds to.

14
Why this wins

Retention is the game, and the design is built around it.

  • Useful before the first log. Population priors make the app accurate on day one, so value lands in session one.
  • Effort is one tap. The timer is the log, the only logging burden this audience tolerates.
  • Nothing can be lost. No streaks, no red, no decay. Punitive mechanics are the documented reason this audience churns.
  • Three return reasons, not one: daily plan, curiosity, and an automatic nudge when their pace shifts.
  • Both platforms at launch. The nearest competitor is iPhone only, and Android is roughly half the addressable audience.
