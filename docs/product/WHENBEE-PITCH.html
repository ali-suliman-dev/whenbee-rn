01
The problem

Some brains are wrong about time. Consistently.

  • "Fifteen minutes" is really forty. Every time. It never gets better on its own.
  • The plan collapses by 11am, and the day turns into apology management.
  • People read it as laziness or not caring. It isn't. It's a broken internal clock.
  • Called time blindness in ADHD, and the planning fallacy in everyone else.
Voice of the user
"I'm always late and people think I'm lazy. I just want to know how long things actually take me."
Recurring phrasing across ADHD app reviews and Reddit, review-mining pass, May 2026.

Why it matters commercially: the problem is already self-diagnosed. People search for it in these exact words, so there is almost no market education to pay for.

Problem
02
Why nothing has fixed it

Every other app guesses for you. None of them learn you.

Type 1
AI estimates
An AI says "this takes 25 minutes." That is a guess about an average person. It is exactly as wrong on your 100th task as on your first.
Type 2
Estimate vs actual reports
"You planned 30, you spent 52." True, and useless. Nothing carries that gap forward into tomorrow's plan.
Type 3
Capacity warnings
"Your day is too full" based on the numbers you typed. But your numbers are the thing that's broken.

All three treat your estimate as data. Whenbee treats your estimate as a symptom, and measures the error.

Problem
03
The solution

One loop. Four seconds of effort a day.

01
Guess
"Emails, 15 min." One tap on a rough-time chip. No forms.
02
Start
One tap. The timer is the log, so there's nothing to remember later.
03
Learn
Stop. Whenbee stores the real number and updates your bias for that kind of task.
04
Honest number
Next time you guess 15, it quietly shows ~25. Your number, not an average.

The whole loop runs on the phone, offline, in about forty lines of math. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Product
07
The design rule that decides retention

It can never make you feel bad. That's a constraint, not a slogan.

Banned
Streaks
Miss a week, nothing breaks and nothing dies. The most-quoted reason people rage-quit ADHD apps.
Banned
Red numbers
Running over shows amber and a neutral line. You were never failing, you were measuring.
Banned
Anything that can go down
Progress only climbs. Once an area is "sealed", it stays sealed forever.

This is enforced in the code, not the marketing. Punitive mechanics lift week-one numbers and then accelerate churn in exactly this audience. Every mechanic in Whenbee has to be impossible to lose.

Design
08
Trust

Everything that matters stays on your phone.

No account
You open it and use it. No email, no password, no sync to break.
No cloud in the loop
Your tasks, timings and multipliers never leave the device. Nothing to leak.
No AI doing the math
Plain arithmetic you could check by hand. Same input, same answer, every time.

Also a business advantage: no servers means near-zero cost per user, so gross margin sits around 85% and pricing is a positioning choice rather than cost recovery.

15
The honest risk

Retention is the whole game, and we know it.

  • The hard part: the discipline this product needs is the exact thing the user struggles with. So logging has to be nearly free, and it is: one tap.
  • The counter: day-one estimates mean the app is useful before you've logged anything.
  • The gate: if week-one retention doesn't reach 25%, we fix the loop before spending a cent on growth.
  • Three reasons to come back, not one: the daily plan, the curiosity of seeing your patterns, and a quiet nudge when your pace changes.
